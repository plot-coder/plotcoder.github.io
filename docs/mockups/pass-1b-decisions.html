Pass 1b: the four decisions, drawn

Drawn 2026-09-26, late, from the twenty-seven items pass 1b held for Robert (`docs/to-do-pass-1b.md`, the list at its foot). The four that shape the app rather than a reply: what a script carries of the wall's opens (entries 41, 42, 37), the page's form for a maybe (34; pass 1a's 34 and 35), the word checks' threshold (31, 32) and what the reading of the whole should also say (45, 46, 47). Each below as it ships beside what it could be, on "Last Orders" as the pass left it, with the question. Pass 2a, run an hour later from the person's side, bears on the first and the last: a person cannot tell from a printed script what is undecided either, and cannot tell from the wall which half of the film is written.

1 · What a script carries of the wall's opens (41, 42, 37)

As it ships. Of everything held open on the wall, two things reach a printed script: an open change line ("[Unwritten] What changes is open, by the writer's word: …") and an open place ("PLACE NOT DECIDED:" as the heading). An open cast, an open when, a maybe, a line about the film, a person's open, a version behind and a cut scene print as nothing, and no file says there is more. The producer's Markdown of "Last Orders" reads as a finished draft with two undecided lines in it; the wall holds fifteen.
What it could be. Every script ends with one page, What is not decided, in the writer's own words, laid out as the reading lists them — about the film, about a person, then card by card — and one line each for a scene held two ways and a scene set aside. The pages themselves stay as they are: a maybe or an open when is not put into a heading a reader might take for a fact. The page prints in Markdown and plain text, and as a last scene-less page in Fountain and Final Draft, so the agency's copy carries it too; set_title_page gains a switch to leave it off a draft that goes out clean.

## What is not decided

*In the writer's words, from the wall, 2026-09-26. Nothing here is on the pages.*

**About the film**

- Tommy Reardon: whom he matters to, I don't know yet.
- Priya and the range, the eleven and the queue: whether those are plants, I don't know yet.
- The logline: I don't know yet — leave it open.

**About the people**
- Mairead Doyle — she is the only one who does not know it yet. Or she knows and will not say. Her husband: I don't know yet.
- Declan Doyle — has he already signed something?

**Scene by scene**
- 1 · The first Friday of the winter — whether Declan Doyle and Priya Nair are in it.
- 2 · The man from the chain — when; anyone else, I don't know yet.
- 3 · The range goes out — whether Mairead Doyle and Declan Doyle are in it.
- 4 · Tommy Reardon's funeral tea — what changes; when; half the town is there and I could not tell you who.
- 5 · The pier: with Priya, her husband's fish knife — what changes. Held two ways: "The pier: alone, the man's card" is set aside behind it.
- 6 · The last night of service — whether Declan Doyle is in it.
- Set aside, not in the film: "The bank: Declan and the manager".

The wall's own list, printed once, at the end, in the writer's words. A producer sees in one page what the wall holds; nothing on the scenes changes. The same page as plain text, in Courier, after the last scene; in Fountain and Final Draft as a page headed the same way.

2 · The page's form for a maybe (34; pass 1a's 34, 35)

As it ships. A card can say "Declan Doyle?" on its cast line. A page cannot: the agent wrote the first Friday with Mairead and nobody else, and put a [[note]] that this was not a decision. A reader of the page sees Declan absent. Pass 1a met the same wall with Ciara at the kitchen and Mallow, and wrote "(listens)".
What it could be. The page keeps the maybe the way the card does: a person who may be in the scene is named once, at the top of the scene's action, in a form that prints and reads as a question. Then the check that reads pages against cast (2a's 45, and the absence check) can count a maybe on the page as the card counts it.

A · a line under the heading, as the page prints it

DOYLE'S - THE FIRST FRIDAY OF THE WINTER

Declan Doyle? Priya Nair? — not decided whether they are here.

The front. Dark by half five, the sea somewhere past the railings, wind coming off it and nothing between.

Over a door, red neon: DOYLE'S. The Y is dead. The rest of it buzzes.

The card's own cast line, printed as the first line of action, in the card's own form: the name and the question mark. It prints in every format, it is one line, and it goes when the cast is decided — write_scene and the exports put it there from the card, never from the text, so it is never stale. The camera check leaves it alone. A reader knows the scene may have two more people in it.

B · a cue that asks · not recommended

                    DECLAN (?)
          (if he is here)
          Mam.

A line of dialogue for a person who may not be there. It invents a line the writer has not licensed, and the cue would count in the cast check as if decided. This is what an agent reaches for when the page has no other form, and it is wrong for the same reason "(listens)" was.

Recommend A. The maybe is the card's; the page borrows it as one printed line drawn from the card at export and at write, so it is always what the wall says. Cost: one line in each export's scene head and in read_pages, tests, and a sentence in the guide's Pages section.

3 · The word checks' threshold (31, 32)

4 · What the reading of the whole should also say (45, 46, 47)

As it ships. "What the app says of the script against the wall" is the pages head: the word checks, the quoted line under each setup, the logline's count. The agent read the rest for itself: who is on the pages, which stretch of the film is written and which guess, what recurs. Pass 2a's person could not tell from the wall that the film was written at all without opening Pages.
What it could be. Two fact lines in the pages head, words not sense, like the ones there: who is on the written pages, by cue, against their cards; and which stretch is written, as the runs are read. A duplicate check over pages — two scenes opening on the same line — is a third, and a design of its own.

written, by stretch: "The first Friday of the winter" → "The last night of service": 4 of 4 cards written, measured; after "The last night of service": 1 of 1. Every scene is written; the runs are measured, not guessed
open, blank, set aside …

The first line is the absence check's page-side twin: it counts cues, which are words, and says where a person is on a card and not on its page (Father Lyons, on the funeral tea's card and given no cue by the agent). The second says of each run whether its pages are measured or guessed, which is what "which half is written" means on a wall. Both are facts; neither asks. The sheet shows the first as a line under the cast lens, the second on the strip.

Two scenes that open on the same sentence. Pass 1b's agent did it on purpose and said the wall could not see it. A question of the duplicate's kind, over first lines only, so it never reads two pages for sense. Worth having, and separate from the two fact lines: it asks, they do not.

Recommend A now, B after. The two fact lines are the pass's own question answered in the reading's own idiom, and 2a's person needs the second on the wall. The echo check is a good question and a smaller one. Cost of A: cue counting is in camera.js already; a run's measured share is in the reading's runs; two lines in the server and the tab, tests, and the guide's sentence on the pages head.

The question

Is this the best we can do? For each: the alternative that loses is drawn beside it. The last page (1A) over marks in the headings (1B), because the pages should read as pages; the card's maybe printed as one line (2A) over an invented cue (2B); a third and four words (3) over none or half, because it asks the one real case and not the two false ones; the two fact lines (4A) first and the echo check (4B) after, because the fact lines answer what both passes asked and the check is a question of its own. All four are words, never sense, and the person sees each of them: the last page in every export, the maybe's line on the page, fewer questions on the sheet, and two lines in Read the wall.

What Robert decides:
  • 1 — the last page, and whether it prints on the agency's Final Draft too (I say yes, with a switch on the title page to leave it off a clean draft);
  • 2 — the maybe's line under the heading, and its words ("not decided whether they are here");
  • 3 — a third and four words; or keep half; or a fact line and no question;
  • 4 — the two fact lines now; the echo check as its own item after.
