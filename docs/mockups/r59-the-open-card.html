The open card (R59): a card that says "not decided"

From round seventeen, the first round to start from a page of notes instead of a treatment. Every record on the wall is an assertion: a card has a place or none, a change line or none, a spot in the order. A writer with an idea has cards that are not any of those things yet — "Declan wants him to move to Naas; where and whether Ruth is there, I don't know" — and the wall can hold that only as an absence the reading then asks about, or as an invention. Below, the same card three ways beside what ships, then the reading and the tool.

As it ships

Declan wants Con to sell the house and move to Naas
Con hears it and says he will think about it.

The change line is the agent's guess, because the field is required. The place is blank, so the reading asks "where does it happen?" every read until the writer answers, and the writer's answer is "I don't know yet".

A · the word on the edge

Open · where, and whether Ruth is there
Declan wants Con to sell the house and move to Naas
What changes?

The writer's own words for what is open, on the top edge where the fold and the payoff already say their word, in the warm colour the wall uses for a debt. The change line can stay the placeholder: an open card is allowed one. Reads at Fit zoom; the edge holds about twenty characters.

B · a sketch of a card

Open · where, and whether Ruth is there
Declan wants Con to sell the house and move to Naas
What changes?

A with the paper itself saying so: a dashed edge and a paler face, the way a pencilled card looks beside inked ones. From across the room you can count the sketches. The same edge line says what is open; the same gesture sets it.

C · a fifth line

Declan wants Con to sell the house and move to Naas
What changes?

Its own line, typed like the cast and the place. Holds a whole sentence. Costs a line on every open card, and does not read from far away; a wall of ideas is mostly open cards.

The gesture

On the card, the corner's picker already asks "fold it, or pay off a fold"; it gains a third line, leave it open, which asks for the writer's words in the edge and stamps the card. Tapping the edge's word edits it; clearing the words closes the card. For the agent, set_open with ids and open (the writer's words; "" closes), and create_note takes open, so a card born from a maybe is born open.

An open card is not asked about. The reading lists it under its own head with the writer's words and asks nothing else of it — not its place, not its change line, not its arrows. It is still counted, still in the order, still a page. When the words are cleared the questions come back on their own, the way a left question does. It is the per-card twin of leave_question: that one leaves a question, this one leaves a card.

Recommended: B, the sketch with the edge line, one field on the card (open, the writer's words or empty), one tool and a line in create_note. A card that is not decided should look undecided from across the room, which A alone does not give and C gives least; and the edge is already where a card says its claims — plants, pays off, the number — so "open" belongs there too. The reading treats it as the writer's word, the twin of a left question: listed, not asked. The paper's colour is untouched (R16).

Is this the best we could do? Two things B cannot say. The edge holds about twenty characters, so the writer's words are short or clipped, and the whole sentence lives in list_board and the tooltip. And an open card silences every question about itself, including ones the writer might still want — an open card with no arrows will not be asked what links it; if that is too blunt, the words could name what is open and the reading skip only that, but then the writer is filling in a form. I would start blunt: open means "leave the whole card alone".
