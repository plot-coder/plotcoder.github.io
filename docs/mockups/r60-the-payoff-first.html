From rounds seventeen and eighteen, on "The Allotments". The writer knows the bucket is in the last scene — Ruth keeps the crowns in it on a balcony — and the key changes hands the morning after the break-in. Where either is first seen, the writer does not know. Recommended: A, the corner folded in over nothing, the writer's words on the edge, one field on the payoff card. It is R58's receiving corner and R59's words put together, and both already exist on the card, so the wall gains no new mark and the picker gains one line. The record lives on the payoff because that is the end the writer has: a plant is the fold's claim, and this is the payoff's — the same thread read from the other end, and the setup arrow is what joins them, as it always was. The reading gains no new kind: "no payoff without its fold" already asks about a payoff whose card is not folded, and now asks about a payoff whose plant is not on the wall. B is the honest picture of a thread and the wrong number of models; C is what the agent did in round eighteen, and it silences the one question the writer wants asked.

Is this the best we could do? Three things A cannot say. The edge holds about twenty characters, so while the debt stands it carries the writer's words alone, in the warm colour, and the corner says "pays off"; "not yet planted" is said by the reading and the tooltip, not the card, and once the plant lands the edge is R58's line and the name leaves it; a card that pays off two unplanted things — the key and the bucket in one scene — gets one edge line and one set of words, and the reading can ask about each only if the words are written "the key; the bucket", which is R58's limit again. The fold's card borrows the thing's name from the payoff, so a fold with no payoff still has no words of its own: "Plants · paid off in 9" stays unnamed until the far end says what; if that reads wrong, the fold could take words too, and then the same thread has two places to be named. And a subplot is not a plant: Declan's thread has no payoff to fold in from, and A leaves him where he was — colour and a group. I would build A and leave the subplot until a round asks for it by name.
