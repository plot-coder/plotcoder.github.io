A thread tied at both ends

The combine log's rule stands and gains its other half. When a thread's start is tied to a card whose fold is free, the picker's "tie it here" folds the card, names the fold from the thread, and draws the setup arrow: one record, the fold's, and the thread lists as before. When the card's fold is another thing's, the thread stays a thread — tied at both ends, listed, asked about nothing — and no arrow is drawn; the reply says so. The guide says the same in one sentence, so the agent that stopped in round nineteen does not have to.
