The words sheet stays in the bar; the combine log records the two doors.

Recommended: B, the bar's words button grown into Help, with the one sheet drawn above. "What does this word mean" and "how do I" are one question, and the app already has a door for it with thirty-four answers behind it; a fifth pill top right would be a second door to the same place and a row in the combine log. The sheet is the same in either home, so A is one button away if a round or a writer says the bar is not where they looked. The search is client-side over the page the site serves, so it costs nothing to host and nothing leaves the device until the writer presses Ask. The ask is one table with a policy of its own, two tools behind a maintainer's sign-in, and a scheduled agent, so that a question asked is a question answered.

Is this the best we could do? Three things to say. The search is words matching words over ten sections; while the guide is that size it is enough, and the day it is not, the sheet's list of unanswered searches — which the ask table can also hold, without an email — says which words the guide lacks. A question sent is a writer's words on our account: the fine print says who reads it and that the answer carries no name, signed out cannot ask, and the table's policy lets a writer read only their own. And a list nobody works is worse than no list: the scheduled agent is part of the build, not an afterthought, and until it runs the sheet should say how long an answer takes. I would build B with the sheet as drawn, the table, the two tools, and the weekly agent.
