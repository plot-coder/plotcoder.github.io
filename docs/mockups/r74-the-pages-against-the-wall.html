R74: the pages read against the wall

Drawn 2026-09-26 from pass 1a (`blind-runs/pass-1a-report.md`, section 6 and entries 68, 71, 72, 73, 85, 88). With all eighteen scenes of "Ninety-Nine" written and the ending rewritten on the writer's word — Noreen scraps it, Joe takes the two thousand, the chime never plays — the wall still said the chime pays off, printed the logline and moved on, and could not see that the ninth turn undid the eighth. Every question about the film as a whole was answered by the agent reading ten pages beside the wall, because no tool reads the one against the other. What follows is the reading's new head as it would print in read_wall and stand in the app's sheet, on the wall as the pass left it, beside what ships; then the rules each check reads by; then the question. Everything here is a question and never a verdict (D21): the app counts words and asks, it does not read for sense.

1 · The moment: after "new ending", what the reading says

As it ships. The agent rewrote the last scene with write_scene and gave the card a new headline. The wall's reading afterwards: the chime's setup arrow still stands and the setups line still says it pays off on that card; the logline prints; the only question is a duplicate, because the new headline shares its place and its sum with the refusal card. Nothing the app said touched the page.
With R74. Once a scene is written, the reading holds its page against what the wall claims of it, and says what it found in the wall's own idiom: a fact where the words land, a question where they do not. The questions are findings like any other — they carry ids, leave_question leaves them, only: "questions" counts them, the sheet has show me on them — printed under their own head so a reader knows they are about pages, not cards.

The same rewrite, as the pass actually had it

Honest about the words. In the pass, the rewritten page did name the chime — it said "the chime does not play" (entry 71). A word check finds "chime" on that page and asks nothing; the plan's example in `docs/to-do-pass-1a.md` ("its page does not say chime") was wrong on that point. What R74 does with that page is the fact line, not the question:
  - "The yard: the letter from the bank" sets up "Noreen's yard: Joe takes the two thousand" — the chime, about 8 pages later (p. 1 → p. 10); on the page: "Noreen starts the van to move it in. The chime does not play."

The setups line quotes the page's own sentence where the plant's word lands, so an agent or a person reads "does not play" in the reading itself, in one call, and decides. The app found the word; the person reads the sense. That is the whole of what R74 claims to do, and it is what the pass lacked: the join of wall and page made by hand, ten pages at a time.

3 · The rules: what each check reads, when it asks, when it is quiet, when it cannot read

All four read words the way the duplicate check does (words() in `readWall.js`): lower-cased, punctuation out, the filler list out, the cast's names out, and no stemming — "plays" and "play" are two words to this reading, and the question says so by listing the words it found. Only a written scene is read. Each is pure, in the kernel, with a test.

Why the logline is a fact line and not the fourth question. R74 as proposed asks about "a logline whose words the last scene does not touch". Run on the treatment's own ending — Joe hands over the keys, the chime plays, Joe laughs, which answers the question the film asks — the check finds one word of six (van) and would ask. A logline is a question about the film and its words are rarely a page's words; the check would ask on most finished scripts, and a question that is always asked is noise. So the logline gets the count and the words found, printed where the logline prints, and the person or agent reads the sense from there. If Robert wants it asked anyway, it is one line to make it a finding with the [unanswered] kind.
What R74 does not read, on purpose. A turn undone by the next one (entry 73) is sense, not words: the eighth turn's page and the ninth's share every word that matters. No word check can ask it and no check should pretend to. What the app can do is make the join one call — the change lines of consecutive turns are already on the wall; the guide gets a line that says, after a rewrite, read the turn against the one before it yourself, and read_wall's "since your last reading" names the rewritten cards so you know which. That is the agent's reading; R74 gives it the pages' facts to read from.
Where it prints. One head, "the pages, against the wall", inside "questions the wall raises" in read_wall, and the same head as a section on the sheet — not a separate tool. The questions are findings, so everything that handles a finding handles these unchanged: the at-a-glance count, only: "questions", leave_question, ask_again, the sheet's show me, the write tails' "the wall's questions have changed since your last read_wall". The kinds go into CHECKS, CHECK_WORDS, ASK_WORDS and the sheet's KIND_NAMES, and the guide's list of what the reading asks grows three lines and the two fact lines.

The question

Is this the best we can do? The alternatives, and why they lose: leave it to the agent — what happened in the pass, and the friction the pass was for: ten pages read beside the wall by hand for every question about the whole, with nothing in the app to check the answer against. A judgement of meaning — an agent inside the app reading pages for sense; not the app's: it counts and asks (D21), and a verdict it cannot show its working for is one a writer cannot trust or leave. A record of rewrites — a flag on a card whose page was rewritten under a claim, asked until left: it would have caught the chime and the ninth turn, but it asks about every rewrite of every claimed card forever until the writer says leave it, and it needs a new field for what the reading last saw; the "since your last reading" line already names the rewritten cards, which is the fact it carries. I recommend the word checks: three questions, two fact lines, the page's own sentence quoted under each setup. They are the duplicate check's kind of reading, honest about being words, pure and testable, and they turn the pass's ten-page join into one call.

What Robert decides:
  • the three questions' words, and their names on the sheet — "A payoff not on the page", "The card behind its page", "A want no page says";
  • the logline as a fact line and not a question (I say fact: see 3); or asked as [unanswered] on his word;
  • "fewer than half" as the line for the change line and the want (the sag's kind of threshold, relative; the question always prints the count, so the writer sees the reading); or "none of its words", which is quieter and would have missed the last card here;
  • whether the quoted page line under each setup is wanted in the app's Read the wall tab as well as in read_wall (I say both: the tab is the person's copy of the same reading);
  • whether a turn read against the one before it stays the agent's reading, with the guide's line and the "since" line's names, or waits for a design of its own.
