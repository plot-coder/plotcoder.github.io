R78: a scene's day, and its light

Drawn 2026-09-27 from pass 4a (blind-runs/pass-4a-report.md, entries 8, 10, 25, 43, and its question 3). The premise of "Low Water" runs six weeks; every card holds a time of day and no card says which day. A reader shooting from the brief asked for the day, the weather and the light of every scene, and the only field near them, the when, holds a decided time that opening for the weather would erase. What follows is the card as it ships, three ways it could carry the day and the light, what the pages and the brief would print, the tools, and the question. The reading asks nothing of either: they are the writer's facts, never the app's questions (D21).

1 · The moment: what the card holds, and what the reader asked

As it ships. The card's place line reads "at INT. THE HARBOUR OFFICE · DAY". The heading prints from it. The brief prints it. Nothing says day four, or rain, or that the light is going. The writer who knows all three has nowhere to put them but the page's action lines, where a check cannot find them and a brief cannot print them apart.
With R78. The card holds two more things in the writer's words: the day — "day four", "the Friday after the crack", "the same night" — and the light — the weather and the hour's light, "rain on the window", "the light going". Each has its own open, as the place and the when do (R61), so "day: not decided — R." stands beside a decided DAY and nothing is erased. The heading stays a heading; the day and the light print beneath it as a note in the pages and the brief.

Open, it carries the mark and the writer's words, as the place and the when do. A person types "day four · rain"; the dot splits them, as it splits the place from the when.

Nothing new on the card: the when is free text and takes it all. The heading then prints "DAY, DAY FOUR, RAIN", which no script does; the brief cannot tell the time from the day from the weather; and "weather not decided" has nowhere to stand beside a decided DAY (entry 43). Rejected.

NOT ON THE WALL: which day of the film's time a scene falls on; what a place looks like beyond its name; a face, a build or a voice beyond the page's line.

The day and the light take their place on the scene line between the time of day and the length, and the NOT ON THE WALL line stops naming the day. A tool reads "day: open" and does not invent one.

The reading asks nothing. No "which day is this?" on every card of a wall that never needed one: a feature set on one afternoon has no days. The reading lists the days once it holds any — "days: day four (3 cards), the Friday after (2), open (1)" beside "places" — so a series' calendar can be read off the wall, and that is all. The only question the wall could ask is the order's: a card on "day six" that follows one on "day nine". That is a check worth its own line in the requirement if the days ever read as numbers; not drawn here.

What it costs. Four fields on the card, two more opens for the last page to list ("What is not decided": "day: not decided — R." under the scene), a fourth line on the card face, one more line on the card's part of the writer's guide, and two tools. What it does not touch: the heading, the count, the order, the checks.

Recommended: A. The day and the light are the scene's, and the scene is the card, so they show on the card as its place and time do, one dimmer line, with the open the place and the when already have. B erases a decided time to open the weather and prints nonsense in a heading. C keeps the card clean by hiding a decision from the person who made it.

Is this the best we could do? The one thing A cannot say is the calendar as a whole: "day four" on forty cards is forty facts, not a timeline. If the days ever read as numbers, the wall could draw them across the top of the story map and ask about a day out of order — a second piece, on its own paper, after this one has been used on a real series. The light is the smaller half of this: it could wait, and the day go first. Say which.
