R80: the walkthrough — the look, the people, the places, then the shots

Drawn 2026-09-27 after the rehearsal on "Ada's column". It replaces section 3 of r80-a-scenes-shots.html. Robert's findings, in his words: without reference characters made first "it's hard to get the images to look like the reference characters"; getting pictures out and back is "screenshotting and reposting"; the app "needs to walk the user through creating these characters, downloading them and naming them correctly" with "really easy copy and paste sections where the user then marks something is done and potentially even uploads the reference files"; and "this has to be done before prompts can be iterated."

1 · One sheet, four steps, in order

Shots in the bar opens the sheet for the project. The four steps stand across its head with what is done. A step opens when the one before it is done; the shots of a scene open as soon as the people and the place that scene's shots name are done, so one scene can be worked to the end before another's people exist.

A person with nothing on their page under looks has no prompt: the row says "Say what Nell looks like first", with the line to type it in, and the words go to her page. The app invents no face.

The two ways a reference is done

A · The picture is in the app (recommended where you can). Dropped, chosen or pasted. The app can then show it beside every shot it belongs to, hand it back to you with its name, and later hand it to a video tool.
B · A tick: "I have it, outside the app." The step is done and the shots open. The app shows the file's name beside each shot and no picture. This is the one that keeps the app light; it costs nothing to store.

Both are drawn because you said marks something is done and potentially even uploads. A tick can become a picture later by dropping one on the row.

Names

A person is their name: ada-quill.png. A place is its phrase: the-harbour-office.png. A shot is its scene's number, its own, and its id: s04-03-n34s.png, so a folder sorts in story order and the app still knows the file if the numbers move. A second try adds -2. A file dropped with its name finds its row; any other file goes to the row it is dropped on.

What is held back

Your own wording. When you change a prompt in your image tool to get the still you keep, the app does not know. A line under a finished shot, "What I changed", is cheap to add; say if you want it.
Video. Still question 26. When a tool is chosen, a shot with a still gets one more button.

3 · Is this the best we can do?

The gate is per scene, not per film. "Before prompts can be iterated" could mean every person in the film before any shot. That is forty references before the first still, and a reason to give up. The gate drawn is: a shot opens when the people it names and its place are done.
Done means a picture or a tick, for references; a picture only, for a shot. A reference kept outside the app still does its job in your image tool. A shot's still is what the app hands on, so it has to be here.
What it cannot fix. The download from your image tool and the upload back into it are that tool's. The app's part is that nothing is retyped: the prompt, the file's name and the list of what to attach are each one copy.
Untested. "Identical faces and build to the references" is a line from a guide, not from our rehearsal. If it does not help in your hands it comes out.

What a yes means. "Build it as drawn" builds the sheet with its four steps, the per-scene gate, the tick for references, the names, paste and drop, the strip, and the writer's guide for it; the agent's tools say the same order and the same names. "No tick" makes a reference done only by a picture in the app. "Gate the whole film" opens the shots only when every person and place in the film is done. Anything else, say what, and it is drawn again first.
