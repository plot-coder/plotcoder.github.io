As it ships · a block in ~/.claude.json, by hand

"mcpServers": {

The desktop app rewrites this file, and three rounds started with the block gone. The server itself is fine; the wiring lives in one file on one machine, and the app's own settings do not know it.

— or, in the desktop app: Settings › Connectors › Add custom connector › the URL —

R48's hosted door already exists in the package (serve) and the repo has a Dockerfile; nothing to build but the deployment. A remote server can be a connector in the desktop app's own settings, which the app keeps and syncs, instead of a block in a file it rewrites. The wiring that vanished is the stdio kind; a URL is the kind the app was built to hold.

What it cannot say: the Basic header carries the writer's password (base64, not encrypted) in the app's settings, the same secret the env block carries today, over https; a writer sharing a machine should know. And a remote server is one more thing that can be down; the stdio door stays as the fallback and the on-ramp says both.

5 · A written scene's lines the camera cannot see (call 6; 18:44)

As it ships · the page passes, the reminder stands

THE ALLOTMENTS

Ruth stands there with the wrong tools. Con knows what she means. He unlocks the shed. She feels the cold of the morning.

read_wall: … checked and clean: no card without a headline or change line … (nothing about the text)
list_reminders: 5. Write for the camera and the cut — …

No check reads prose. The house principle is in the reminders every project starts with, and a writer's agent is expected to keep it. Eighteen's agent wrote three such lines from the notes' facts and noted that nothing flagged them.

After · read_pages marks them, the wall asks nothing

read_pages:
1  THE ALLOTMENTS  — 1/8 page, written  · camera: 2 lines it cannot see (knows, feels)

  Ruth stands there with the wrong tools. Con knows what she means.   ◂ knows
  He unlocks the shed. She feels the cold of the morning.             ◂ feels

write_scene reply: … 2 lines the camera cannot see (knows, feels): the reminder "Write for the camera" is the house's; show it or cut it, on the writer's word.

A small list of interior verbs in action lines — knows, feels, thinks, remembers, realises, wants, decides, understands — marked where the page is read and counted in the write's reply. Not a question on the wall: the wall's questions are about the story's shape, and a sentence on a page is the writer's. Dialogue is never read; only action.

Recommended: the marks on the page and in the write's reply, no wall question. It costs a word list and two lines of reading, and it puts the house principle where the writer can see it act. What it cannot say: "She knows the way to the shed" is a fact the camera can show and it would be marked; the list will be wrong sometimes, which is why it marks and never asks. If that is too much noise, the default — leave it — stands and costs nothing.

7 · A written sketch that shrinks the wall (18:43, 19:47, 20:49, 21:37)

Four rounds wrote the first morning from the facts alone — three silent lines — and watched the film's first turn become an eighth of a page. The measure is the truth (R23 b, D23), the reply says why, and the estimate is kept underneath; but a sketch reads as a finished eighth.

After · a sketch is named, and the wall says both numbers

write_scene: Wrote "The first morning": 3 line(s) as they print, measured at 1/8 of a 55-line page — a sketch: shorter than the page it was read as; the wall counts the measure and says so.

No new record and no change to the count: a written card whose measure is under the estimate it replaced (the writer's, or the page an unsized card is read as) is called a sketch, and the runtime line carries the second number beside the first. When the scene is written out and passes its page, the word goes. The measure stays the truth; the reading stops pretending the sketch is the scene.

Recommended: build it — a word and a second number, no record. It is what four agents asked for in the same breath, and it keeps D21: the app counts and says which count it is. What it cannot say: a deliberately short scene — a one-line beat — reads as a sketch until the writer sizes it at an eighth, which is one call; and "about 11 if it ran to that" is a guess about a guess, said as one.
