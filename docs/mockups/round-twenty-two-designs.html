1 · Adding one scene re-lays the whole wall (E4 · entry 63)

What happened. The writer said "add a scene between the breakdown and the mother coming to the stop". One card was added, and every card on the wall moved, because wiring a scene into the story (create_note with after, and move_scene) finishes by tidying the whole wall along the arrows. Rounds fourteen and sixteen asked for that tidy — back then a card's position was the story's order, so an untidy wall read wrong. Since then the order is the arrows (R56), and the tidy is only housekeeping. If you had arranged the wall by hand, it is gone.

The story's order is already right the moment the arrows are drawn; where cards sit is the writer's. The same for move_scene and the new set_order. An agent building a whole wall calls organize once at the end, which is what the guide already tells it to do.

What I would do: A. The reason for the tidy went away with R56, and a tool that moves nine of your cards to add one is surprising in a way no reply can excuse. What it costs: a wall built card by card by an agent is untidy until organize runs, and a card dropped beside its neighbour can overlap another; the reply says so. What a yes means: wiring a scene in, moving one, and setting the order stop tidying; the reply offers organize; on the wall nothing changes for a person, who never had this tidy.

2 · "A feature" is not a number (E8 · entry 91)

What happened. "The target: I have decided. It is a feature." The tool takes only pages or minutes, so the agent chose the app's 120 and told the writer it had. The record now says the writer chose 120, which they did not. And there is a quieter fault behind it: 120 is also the default, so a wall whose writer has said "a feature" reads back as "no target set".

One word on the board record — targetKind — claiming nothing when absent. The strip's readout shows the word where it shows "target 120". A page count given later replaces it.

What it cannot say: what this writer's feature runs to — 90, 100, 120 — which is why the reply says it is read as 120 and how to say otherwise. What a yes means: set_target and new_project take kind; the readout, the reading and the title page say the word.

3 · How a plant pays off, as against where (F1 · entries 14, 35, 54)

What happened. The writer knew how the jar pays off — "he empties it into her hand" — and later how it is first seen — "he counts the fare out of it on the step of the bus". A setup arrow says where a plant pays off and the fold's words say what is planted; nothing says how. The first sentence went into the last scene's headline, the second into the fold's label, where it now repeats in full on every line that names the fold.

A field on the setup arrow, shown on the payoff card's edge as "Pays off · the jar — he empties it into her hand".

B · no field: say where "how" goes

The guide, under the fold: How a plant is first seen, and how it pays off, are what happens in those two scenes — so they are those cards' change lines, or their text. The fold's words stay a short label ("the jar of coins"): it is repeated wherever the fold is named.

"He empties it into her hand" is what changes in the last run; "he counts the fare out of a jar" is the first morning. The wall already has a place for a sentence about what happens in a scene.

What I would do: B, the words and no field. A adds a second place to say what happens in a scene, and the app's method is that a card says what changes in it. The agent reached for the label because nothing told it where a sentence goes; with R67, a change line is also no longer something it has to avoid. What B cannot say: a last scene that pays off two plants has one change line for both — a sentence can hold that, and if a round shows it cannot, A is still there. What a yes to B means: two sentences in the guide and in set_plant's description; nothing a person sees changes.

4 · Two versions as equals, and a version that is two scenes (F3 · entries 10, 11)

What happened. "I have it two ways. Keep both on the wall until I decide." The app holds that — one card in front, one tucked behind — but one has to be in front, so the agent asked the writer "which goes in front?", a half-choice the writer had just refused to make. And the breakdown's second way was two places (the bog road, then his home): a version is one card.

A · side by side under one bracket, neither in front

A new shape on the wall: a bracket holding two (or more) cards in one place in the story, counted as one scene — the longer of them — until one is chosen. It could also hold a version that is two cards. It is a second way of drawing R65, and every part of the app that reads the order would learn "a slot with several cards".

B · keep the drawing, fix the asking

The guide and set_alternative: Which card is in front decides nothing: it is only the one drawn on top, and the one counted until the writer chooses. Put the way the writer named first in front, say so, and do not ask them to pick. And: a version is one card. When one way of a scene is two scenes, the version is the first of them; hold the second as a card set aside, and bring it back if that way is chosen.
What I would do: B. The writer's complaint was being asked, not how the cards were drawn; the pair already reads "two versions · not chosen" and nothing in the reading treats the front card as preferred. A is a handsome drawing and a large change — a third kind of place in the story — for one round's remark. What B cannot say: which way the count leans while undecided; it counts the front card, and says so. What a yes to B means: words in the guide and the tool; nothing a person sees changes.

6 · The camera mark, for the writer (F7 · entries 81, 82)

What happened. The writer's own line — "She knows every passenger by their stop. She does not know him." — was marked: the camera cannot see "knows". The agent saw the mark in read_pages; the writer had to ask whether it was a question for them. It turns out the app's own Pages never show the mark at all: the agent is told something about the writer's page that the writer cannot see. And a mark can never be acknowledged — a line kept on purpose is marked for ever.

As it ships · the app's page shows nothing

The bus stops. TOMÁS Ó CEALLAIGH, fourteen, gets on.

 

She knows every passenger by their stop. She does not know him.

read_pages (the agent's): … She does not know him.    ◂ knows

A · the mark in the app's margin

The bus stops. TOMÁS Ó CEALLAIGH, fourteen, gets on.

 

She knows every passenger by their stop. She does not know him.◂ knows

The same mark the agent sees, in the page's right margin where a revision's star goes, with a one-second hover: "The camera cannot see knows. A mark, not a question." Writer and agent look at the same page.

B · and a click keeps the line

The bus stops. TOMÁS Ó CEALLAIGH, fourteen, gets on.

 

She knows every passenger by their stop. She does not know him.◂ kept

Clicking the mark says "I know; it stays", remembered on the card for that line; the mark greys and the agent's page says "kept by the writer". A small record, and the first thing in the app that remembers a line of text.

What I would do: A now, B only if a round or you ask for it. A closes the real gap — that your agent is told something you cannot see. B is a nicety with a cost: remembering a line means deciding what happens when the line is edited. What a yes to A means: the mark and its hover in Pages (the Pages tab and print leave it out: it is a working mark, like the scene numbers' stars, not part of the script).

7 · Two phrases a writer reads (F5, F6 · entries 36, 40)

What happened. Two of the app's own sentences misled. "Propose them and I will strike" is in the list of questions a writer sees under Reminders, filed under marking turns — the agent read it as mark first, then strike; the writer meant name them to me first. And the wall's question about two turns with nothing between them offers three answers, none of which is "I have not got that far" — which, on a page of notes, is usually the truth.

The turns · the hint under "Which scenes are the turns?"

Name them, say "propose them and I will strike", or say "mark none yet" …

Name them; or say "propose them and I will strike" — your agent names its candidates to you first, you strike, and only what is left is marked; or say "mark none yet" — every card stays a scene and the reading asks once for a beat until you do.

Two turns back to back · the wall's question

Nothing runs between "The day Nuala finds out" and "The last run": two turns back to back. Are they one beat, is a scene missing, or is that the pace? is that the pace — or have you not got that far yet?

"Not that far yet" is answered by leaving the question, with those words as the reason, which the wall already does. No new state for a stretch of story.

What I would do: both, as written. They are words, and they are words you read, which is the only reason they are on this page. What a yes means: the two sentences change, in the app and for the agent alike.
