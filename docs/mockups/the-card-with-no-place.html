Recommended: A. What it cannot say: whether the writer has not thought about the place or does not want one (a montage, a voice over black); the second is what the open place's words are for. 2 · A fact about the whole film has no home but the premise (20:9, 20:20)

As it ships · the agent guesses where it goes

20:9   The notes give the film's span, March to October. The app has a when per card and nothing for the story's span. I offered you the premise for it; that was a guess at where it belongs.
20:20  "Con does not die in this film" is in Con's notes because nothing else took it. It's a fact about the film, not about Con before it starts.

Both agents found a home — the premise, a person's notes — and both said they were guessing. The premise is on the project record, prints in the title page's notes, and list_boards and read_project carry it; it is the one text that is about the film and not about a card. Nothing says so — and read_wall, the read an agent answers "what is on this wall" from, prints the premise only when it is open, never when it is set.

After · the guide and the tool say what the premise is for

set_premise — the description, grown by a sentence:
  … The premise is also where a fact about the whole film goes when no card holds it: its span ("March to October"), a rule it keeps ("Con does not die in this film"). One or two sentences each; a fact about one scene belongs on its card.

The agent's guide, under the premise, and writers.html, in the same words for a person:
  What is true of the whole film and of no one scene — when it runs from and to, what never happens in it — goes in the premise.

read_wall, above the logline, when a premise is set:
  premise: "A widower and a stranger share an allotment, March to October. Con does not die in this film."

No new field and no second model: a span derived from the cards' whens would be a claim the app makes, and the whens are mostly open on a young wall; a "rules" list would be the premise under another name. Two sentences, in the three places an agent or a writer looks, and one line in the reading so a fact put there is read back with the wall.

Recommended: the sentences, and nothing else, after round twenty-two. "The Last Bus" has a span too (September to New Year), and its agent has not been told where a span goes; if it reaches for the premise unprompted, the sentence is only a confirmation, and if it guesses somewhere else, the round says where an agent expects to find it, which is better evidence than twenty's alone. What it cannot say: the premise is prose, so the wall cannot check a scene against "Con does not die"; that is a reading for a person or an agent, and D21 would keep it that way.
